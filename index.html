The Process

How Rainwater Harvesting Works

A simple yet powerful 5-step system that turns monsoon rains into a year-round water resource.

01
🏠

Collection Surface

Rainwater falls on rooftops, paved terraces, or open ground. The catchment area determines how much water can be harvested.

02
🌊

Gutters & Pipes

Water channels direct rainwater from the catchment surface through gutters, downpipes, and conveyance systems.

03
🔍

First Flush Filter

The first flush diverter removes polluted initial rainfall, ensuring only clean water enters the storage. Mesh screens filter debris.

04
🪣

Storage Tank

Filtered water flows into underground sumps, overhead tanks, or recharge pits — designed to hold thousands of litres.

05
✅

Use & Recharge

Harvested water is used for domestic needs, irrigation, or groundwater recharge — completing the sustainable water cycle.

From your home to the nation — the ripple effects of rainwater harvesting are transformative.

Economic
🌳

Recharge Groundwater

Environmental

Capturing runoff reduces stormwater overflow, minimizing urban flash floods during monsoon season.

Disaster Risk

Agricultural

System Types

Types of Rainwater Harvesting Systems

Choose the right system based on your needs, location, and available space.

🏠

Rooftop Rainwater Harvesting

The most popular and widely implemented method in urban India. Rainwater collected from rooftops is filtered and stored in tanks or used for groundwater recharge.

  • ✅ Suitable for homes, apartments, and offices
  • ✅ Low installation cost (₹15,000–₹1 Lakh)
  • ✅ Mandatory in many Indian cities (Bengaluru, Chennai, Delhi)
  • ✅ Can supply 40–50% of annual household water needs
  • ✅ Easy to maintain with basic filter cleaning
Cities with mandatory RWH:
Bengaluru Chennai Delhi Hyderabad Pune Kolkata

Surface Runoff Harvesting

Captures rainwater flowing over ground surfaces — fields, roads, and open areas. Ideal for agricultural land and rural areas in India.

  • ✅ Best for rural & agricultural use
  • ✅ Used extensively in Rajasthan (Johads), Tamil Nadu
  • ✅ Can irrigate large land areas
  • ✅ Traditional method — low tech, high impact
  • ✅ Supports dryland farming communities

Groundwater Recharge

Specially designed structures that allow harvested rainwater to percolate into the ground, replenishing aquifers and boosting borewell levels.

  • ✅ Combats groundwater depletion
  • ✅ Used in IT parks, housing societies
  • ✅ Raises borewell water table over time
  • ✅ Subsidy available under government schemes
  • ✅ Reduces land subsidence risk

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about setting up rainwater harvesting in India.

Is rainwater harvesting mandatory in India? +

Yes, rainwater harvesting is mandatory in several Indian cities and states. Cities like Bengaluru (Karnataka), Chennai (Tamil Nadu), Delhi, Hyderabad (Telangana), and Pune (Maharashtra) have made RWH compulsory for new constructions above a certain plot size. Many states offer property tax rebates for households that install RWH systems.

How much does a basic rainwater harvesting system cost in India? +

Costs vary based on system type and scale:
• Basic rooftop system (5,000L tank): ₹15,000–₹30,000
• Medium household system (10,000–20,000L): ₹40,000–₹1,00,000
• Groundwater recharge pit: ₹10,000–₹50,000
• Society/commercial building: ₹2–₹15 Lakhs
Government subsidies can reduce costs by 25–50%.

Is harvested rainwater safe to drink? +

Rainwater is naturally pure as it falls, but it can pick up contaminants from roofs and pipes (bird droppings, dust, pollutants). With proper first-flush diversion and a multi-stage filtration system (sand filter + activated carbon + UV purification), harvested rainwater can be made safe for drinking. Most households use it for non-drinking purposes like gardening, flushing, and washing without treatment.

What is the 'first flush' and why is it important? +

The 'first flush' refers to the initial rainfall that washes dust, bird droppings, leaves, and other pollutants off the roof. This first batch of water is the most contaminated. A first-flush diverter (also called a rain head or first-flush valve) automatically diverts this water away from the storage tank, ensuring only cleaner water — from the second flush onwards — enters your storage system.

How do I calculate the size of storage tank I need? +

Use our calculator above! As a rule of thumb:
Tank size = Daily water consumption × Dry days between rain events
For a family of 4 using ~500 litres/day with 5–7 days between monsoon rains, a 5,000–10,000 litre tank is typically recommended. In cities with longer dry spells, consider 15,000–25,000 litres.

Can I get a subsidy from the government for installing RWH? +

Yes! Multiple central and state schemes provide subsidies:
• Jal Shakti Abhiyan: Technical assistance and material support
• State Water Boards: Many state governments offer 25–50% capital subsidies
• NABARD: Loans for rural water harvesting at concessional rates
• Property Tax Rebates: Several municipalities offer 5–10% rebates on property tax for certified RWH installations
Contact your local municipality or district water board for details.

What maintenance does a rainwater harvesting system need? +

Maintenance is minimal and straightforward:
• Pre-monsoon: Clean gutters, pipes, first-flush diverter, and filters
• Monthly: Check for debris in screens, inspect tank for algae growth
• Annually: Clean the storage tank, check for cracks, replace filter media (sand/gravel) if needed
• As needed: Paint or coat exposed pipes to prevent corrosion
A well-maintained system can last 20–30 years.
